How to log food temperatures for mobile catering

Temperature control is the check EHOs scrutinise most. Here is exactly what to record, the safe limits, and how to log it in seconds as you trade.

Safe temperatures at a glance

StageSafe temperature
Fridge / cold holding8°C or below (aim 0–5°C)
Freezer−18°C or below
Hot holding63°C or above
Cooking / reheating (core)75°C for 30 seconds
Danger zone (avoid)8°C to 63°C
Scotland guidance commonly asks for reheating to 82°C — check with your local authority.

How to log a temperature, step by step

  1. Check the appliance or probe the food.
  2. Enter the reading next to that appliance and save it — about five seconds per check.
  3. If a reading fails (e.g. a fridge running warm), record what you did about it. A corrective action looks better to an EHO than a gap.
  4. Add the fridges, freezers and hot-hold units you actually use, and remove any you don't.

How often should you record?

At least twice a day — once at the start of service and once during — plus checks on deliveries and on cooking high-risk items like chicken and burgers. Two readings a day is the practical minimum; more is better, and logging as you go keeps every entry time-stamped rather than filled in from memory later.

FAQ

What temperature should a food van fridge be?

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland the legal maximum for cold holding is 8°C, but aim for 0–5°C for a safety margin.

How many times a day should I record temperatures?

At minimum twice — start and during service — plus deliveries and cooking checks. Consistent daily records are what an EHO looks for.
