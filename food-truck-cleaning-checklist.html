Food Truck Cleaning Checklist: Daily & Weekly Guide

A practical cleaning checklist for UK food trucks, catering vans, trailers and market stalls — covering what to clean, when to clean it and how to keep a record.

Quick answer: your cleaning schedule should reflect the risks and equipment in your business. The important part is not having a huge checklist — it is having a suitable routine, following it consistently and being able to show what you have done.

What should a food truck cleaning checklist include?

Start with the surfaces and equipment that come into contact with food, then work outwards to the rest of the unit. A useful checklist normally covers:

  • Food preparation surfaces and chopping boards
  • Utensils, knives, containers and food-contact equipment
  • Cooking equipment, grills, fryers and hot-holding equipment
  • Fridges, freezers and door handles
  • Hand-washing facilities, taps and surrounding surfaces
  • Floors, walls and other areas where dirt or food debris can build up
  • Waste bins and waste-storage areas
  • External service areas and customer-facing surfaces

Daily cleaning checklist

Your daily routine should be specific to your operation. A simple mobile catering checklist can include:

  1. Remove food debris and visible contamination from food-contact surfaces.
  2. Clean and disinfect food-contact surfaces using the correct method and product.
  3. Clean utensils and equipment after use and store them so they remain clean.
  4. Clean hand-washing facilities and replenish soap and disposable towels.
  5. Clean floors and deal with spills promptly.
  6. Empty and clean waste bins as required.
  7. Check fridges and other equipment for spills, dirt or damaged seals.
  8. Record the cleaning checks and any corrective action.

Weekly or periodic deep cleaning

Some areas need more than a quick daily clean. Depending on your setup, schedule deeper cleaning of extraction areas, behind and underneath equipment, storage shelves, fridge interiors, vehicle surfaces and other hard-to-reach areas.

Do not simply copy a restaurant checklist. A burger van, coffee trailer and festival catering unit have different equipment and risks. Your schedule should match what you actually use.

Why cleaning records matter

Cleaning is one part of your food safety management system. A record can help demonstrate that your procedures are being followed and can highlight repeated problems that need fixing.

The Food Standards Agency's Safer Food Better Business approach uses practical safe methods and a diary rather than asking small businesses to create unnecessary paperwork. The FSA says businesses should follow the safe methods that apply to them and complete the diary every day. See the FSA SFBB guidance.

Cleaning checklist mistakes to avoid

  • Too generic: “Clean van” does not tell anyone what was actually checked.
  • Too complicated: a checklist nobody completes is not useful.
  • No corrective action: record what happened when something failed or was missed.
  • No frequency: distinguish daily, weekly and task-specific cleaning.
  • Ignoring allergen cross-contamination: cleaning procedures should consider allergen risks as well as general hygiene.
