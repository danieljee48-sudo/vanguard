HACCP for Mobile Caterers UK: A Practical Guide

A plain-English introduction to HACCP for food trucks, catering vans, trailers and market traders — what it is, why records matter and how to keep the system practical.

What is HACCP?

HACCP stands for Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point. It is a systematic way of identifying food-safety hazards, deciding how they are controlled and checking that those controls continue to work.

For a small mobile caterer, HACCP does not need to mean a huge folder of paperwork. The system should be appropriate to the nature and size of the operation.

What hazards should a mobile caterer consider?

The exact hazards depend on your menu and process, but common categories include:

  • Biological: harmful bacteria and other microorganisms.
  • Chemical: cleaning chemicals or other substances contaminating food.
  • Physical: foreign objects entering food.
  • Allergen: incorrect allergen information or cross-contact with allergens.

Map your food process

Start with how food actually moves through your business: receiving ingredients, storage, preparation, cooking, cooling, reheating, hot holding, service and disposal. A mobile business may also have transport and temporary trading-location considerations.

At each stage ask: what could go wrong, what control do we use, and how do we know the control is working?

Where do records fit in?

Records provide evidence that important controls are being monitored and maintained. The FSA's MyHACCP guidance says documentation and record keeping are essential to applying a HACCP system and that records should be appropriate to the nature and size of the operation.

Examples can include temperature monitoring, cleaning checks, corrective actions, delivery information and other records needed to demonstrate that your food-safety controls are being followed.

Don't manufacture paperwork. A useful system records the controls that matter to your actual food process. Your local environmental health team can advise on the requirements for your particular business.

HACCP vs SFBB

SFBB is one practical way smaller food businesses can manage food safety using safe methods and a diary. The FSA describes SFBB as being based on HACCP principles. More complex operations may use a more detailed HACCP-based system or MyHACCP.

The right approach depends on your operation. If your food processes are simple, an FSA pack may be suitable; if your process is more complex, seek appropriate professional or local-authority guidance.

A simple record structure for a food truck

  1. Daily opening and hygiene checks.
  2. Relevant refrigeration and hot-holding checks.
  3. Cooking, reheating or probe checks where your process requires them.
  4. Cleaning and disinfection records.
  5. Delivery and traceability information.
  6. Allergen information and management controls.
  7. Corrective actions when a control fails.
  8. Periodic review of your food safety procedures.

Official guidance

The FSA provides a free MyHACCP tool and guidance on documentation and record keeping. For smaller food businesses, the FSA also says SFBB or Safe Catering packs may provide a simpler approach.
